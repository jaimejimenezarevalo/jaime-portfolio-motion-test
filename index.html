About me

Proof, computation,
and mathematical curiosity.

I am a first-generation undergraduate mathematics student at California State University, Dominguez Hills, where I study mathematics and conduct research in coding theory and combinatorics. My current work includes decoding polar affine Grassmann codes and investigating the relationship between Liar's Bingo and error-correcting codes.

I am especially interested in using proof, computation, and mathematical experimentation to understand abstract structures. Alongside my research, I work as a tutor and supplemental instructor, helping students build confidence in subjects ranging from introductory mathematics to differential equations and linear algebra.

My long-term goal is to earn a Ph.D. in mathematics, contribute to mathematical research, and expand access to academic opportunities for students from underrepresented communities.

Decoding Polar Affine Grassmann Codes

Researching decoding methods for polar affine Grassmann codes through algebraic structure, proof, and computation. This work grew from an NSF REU in combinatorics and coding theory.

02

Liar's Bingo & Error-Correcting Codes

Studying connections between a combinatorial game, coding theory, and error correction. Python simulations model strip construction, introduced errors, and extensions to new bases.

Second place, CSUDH Student Research Conference.
